# The other players check a padel Level

**Status:** accepted. Spec: `.scratch/padel-declared-band-trust/spec.md`. Amends [ADR-0068](./0068-a-football-match-can-ask-for-performance-ratings.md) (the window asks a second question on padel), [ADR-0095](./0095-performance-ratings-move-reliability-and-a-loss-costs-more-below-full.md) (peers now move a padel Level, and a padel Public Game opens a window) and [ADR-0011](./0011-match-result-confirmation.md)'s reversal rule (a Match's Level check is taken back first, and no later check refuses a reversal).

A padel declaration sets the starting φ (ADR-0098), so a low declaration is trusted and a player who said D but plays C climbs slowly on results alone. The owner asked for a third signal: after a Match the other three players say what band the player played at, and their answer is one measurement of the Level, weighed by the player's φ so that it counts most while the Level is new. ADR-0095 had refused to let peers move a padel Level; the owner reversed that, and kept the check on for confirmed players.

## Decision

- **Where it is asked.** On a padel Friendly game on a Verified Group, the Match whose Rating window ADR-0095 opens, and on a padel Public Game whose Match counts for Temba Level (one account holder from each side confirmed within 24 hours, or a Verifier stamp, ADR-0071, ADR-0072), at the Game's own weight (1 on a Club Group and on a Public Game, 0.5 on another Verified Group). A padel Public Game opens the same window a Verified Group does: Everyone mode, 24 hours, answers hidden, the 1 to 10 Performance rating moving Reliability and the Level check, and its four seated account holders are told it is open. One whose score stood without the other side's confirmation moved no Level and opens none. Football windows, tier Groups, tournaments and Challenge Games ask none. Public Games are where strangers meet on a declared Level, so they are asked too; this rests on an owner default not yet confirmed (OD-4 in the spec), and if the owner keeps the check to Verified Groups, Public Games open no window again.
- **The question.** Rate the game also asks "What level did {name} play at?" about each of the other three: the nine display bands D to A in one row, opened on the band the player shows, marked "now". Nothing is selected until tapped, and a blank is not an answer. The opponents are not optional, because the check needs one of them.
- **Answers** (`level_check_ballots`) are their own table, one per Match, rater and player, never about oneself, holding the picked band's lower stored band. They are hidden like Performance ratings: a player reads only their own, and no door shows who answered what, an Organizer and an Operator included. They can be changed or cleared until the window closes (`games.submitLevelChecks`, whole-set semantics), and they are kept through a wrong-score report.
- **Who is checked.** A seated player with a padel Rating, confirmed or not, with at least two answers about them and at least one from the opposing Game team. These two rules count answers, not weights.
- **Who counts half.** A rater weighs 1, an Operator's and a Level setter's answer included, with no extra weight. A rater whose own padel Level is Provisional when the window settles (ADR-0080) weighs 0.5. An outlier, a rater whose answers were on average more than 1.5 display bands from the verdicts in this Match and in each of their previous two Matches with a Level check, weighs half again, so a Provisional outlier weighs 0.25. This Match's distance is read against the verdicts with the Provisional weights alone, and a split verdict sits halfway between its two bands; a rater with fewer than two earlier such Matches is never an outlier. Both weights are worked out when the window settles, from the ledger and the stored answers, as the Performance rating outlier rule is (ADR-0068): no column or table records a judgement of a rater, and no door says whose answer counted for less. The threshold is a starting value, modelled on that rule.
- **The verdict** is the weighted median band. With the weight split evenly across two bands it is the mean of the two bands' middles. A band's middle is the middle of its span on the display face, so D is 0.70, the middle of 0.0 to 1.4.
- **Agreement.** A verdict agrees with the band the player shows when it is that band, or when the weight split evenly across two bands and the shown band is one of them. A split's Level reads as one of its two bands or as one between them (a C and C+ split is 3.50, which reads as C+), so reading the Level alone would agree with a C+ player and move a C player on the same answers.
- **The step.** A verdict that agrees moves no μ and only narrows φ. Otherwise μ moves by `k × (μ_verdict − μ)` with `k = φ² / (φ² + 250²)`, capped at 0.35 Level, then blended by the Game's weight, the weight stored on the Match's rating events (read from the Group only for a Match that has none, so a Group that gains or loses its Venue within the 24 hours changes nothing); φ narrows as by one more measurement, `1 / sqrt(1/φ² + 1/250²)`, blended the same way. The band follows with hysteresis. At weight 1 a one-band gap moves a player at φ 250 by half of it, at φ 150 by about a quarter and at φ 100 by about a seventh. Pure in `@repo/domain/level-check`.
- **It settles with the window**, after Reliability, by the `rating-window.settle` Job or the read-time fallback, locking `ratings` rows in user-id order.
- **Its own ledger** (`level_check_results`): one row per player and Match with the verdict, whether it agreed, the answers counted, the weight and the Rating either side. It is not a rating event: not a Rated Match, so it never counts towards confirming a Level, and it leaves σ, `last_rated_at` and Reliability alone.
- **Reversal.** A wrong-score report takes the Match's Level check back before it restores the Match's rating events: exactly when nothing has touched the Rating since, otherwise by subtracting its μ move, and not at all when a Level override was set since. No later Level check refuses a reversal; a Match with one settled after it has its own μ move subtracted instead of its before-values restored, so the check survives.
- **What the player sees.** On the played Game, "Players placed you at {band}" with the Level before and after, "Agreed with your level", or why nothing settled. A "Level check" line in Rating history. The padel "ratings are in" Notification goes to a player whose rating or Level check settled and reads both.
- **Readers that follow a Level over time** treat the ledger as they treat Place awards. A Standing's Most improved view does not count a Level check as improvement, as it does not count a Level override. Match history keeps a Match row's own move and leaves the check out of it, as Rating history shows the check on its own line. An Unclaimed player merge moves the answers and results with the Rating, or deletes them with it.
- **The Level recalculation (ADR-0100) refuses** once any Level check result exists: it does not replay one.
- **Switched on by the API.** The check is asked only while the API has `PADEL_LEVEL_CHECK_ENABLED=1`. Unset, no window asks it, `games.submitLevelChecks` refuses, settling writes no result, and a padel Public Game opens no Rating window, since that window exists for the check; a padel Friendly game on a Verified Group still opens its window, and its Performance ratings move Reliability as before. Because the recalculation refuses once a check has settled, the code may deploy before the recalculation's production run, and the owner sets the flag only after the dry run that follows the apply reports no changes (`packages/api/README.md`).

**Considered Options**: a three-way answer, lower, same or higher (in the prototype it leaves a player who said D and plays C at 1.42 after nine Matches, against 2.28 with bands); letting agreement move μ towards the band's middle (an honest D drifts up from 0.35 to 0.57); writing the check as a rating event (one per User and Match is unique, and every row counts as a Rated Match towards confirmation); storing the answer on the Performance rating row (that row is deleted when its 1 to 10 is cleared); switching the check off at confirmation (the owner kept it on); refusing a reversal after a later check (a check is additive enough to come off by subtraction, as a Place award does).

## Consequences

- Four players who agree to answer high can each gain up to 0.35 Level a Match on a Club Group. The cap, the opponent rule and φ falling with every check limit it, and the ledger shows every move to an Operator.
- With three raters a verdict says a lot about who answered what, as the 1 to 10 result already does.
- A reversal after a later Level check restores μ by subtraction, not exactly.
- The check starts with the first window that settles after the flag is set, which must follow the Level recalculation's production run.
- A player shown C with answers split between C and C+ keeps their μ, as one shown C+ does; one shown C- with answers split between D+ and C, whose mean reads C-, is moved towards that mean.
- Opening the window on a padel Public Game also brings the 1 to 10 Performance rating and Reliability to it, which ADR-0095 had left out.
- A Public Game's Rated Match never confirms a Level (ADR-0080), and neither does its Level check, so a player who plays only Public Games stays Provisional: the Prove-it rule never ends for them, and their own answers count half.
